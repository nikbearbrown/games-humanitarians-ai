Crystal Palace

Two buildings, one project. The Crystal Palace was built in Hyde Park for the Great Exhibition of 1851, then rebuilt at Sydenham from 1854 onward. Each venue has its own walkthroughs here, and each is built from the surviving plates, drawings and records, so what you see is a reconstruction, not a photograph.

How to read these

A walkthrough you can move through is not the same as a verified reconstruction. The Hyde Park frame comes from Charles Downes's 1852 drawings and covers 37 of 77 bays; it has no glazing, end wall or entrance. Object models are studies and simplified interpretations. Every model page shows the image it was based on when that image's licence is documented, and says so when it is not.

39 more models do not name a venue in their own title, so they are listed separately until each is placed.
